09. Tell the Story with Charts¶

Suggested time: 45–60 minutes. Work through lessons in order; each notebook includes its own runnable examples.

All people, policies, prices, and records are fictional. Amounts use Philippine pesos (₱ / PHP). This is a coding lesson, not financial advice. Real coverage and claims depend on policy terms and applicable law. Never enter personal financial or health information.

Run this lesson in your browser with Google Colab. Sign in to Google if prompted. Use File → Save a copy in Drive to keep your work, then run the cells in order.

Story opening¶

Maya prepares a chart of the same Oak and River policies from lesson 06 for the learning fair. Leo asks whether a rising line of premiums is the same as an account balance or a promised payout.

Learning goals¶

Build numeric sequences; plot labeled lines; interpret axes.

Insurance in everyday words¶

Total premiums paid is the sum of payments. It is not the death benefit or a savings balance. These examples are term policies; a benefit depends on a covered death and policy terms. The graph cannot decide whether a policy is suitable.

Python toolbox¶

range(0, 11) generates years 0 through 10. A list comprehension builds a list with one calculation per year. matplotlib draws a figure: label both axes, include units, and add a legend. Assume premiums stay fixed and all scheduled payments are made.

Predict, then run¶

At year 10, where will the lines for ₱250/month and ₱350/month end?

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
years = list(range(0, 11))
oak_paid = [250 * 12 * year for year in years]
river_paid = [350 * 12 * year for year in years]
fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(8, 4))
ax.plot(years, oak_paid, marker="o", label="Oak: PHP 250/month")
ax.plot(years, river_paid, marker="s", label="River: PHP 350/month")
ax.set(title="Fictional term policies: cumulative premiums", xlabel="Years of payments", ylabel="Total premiums paid (PHP)")
ax.legend()
ax.grid(alpha=0.3)
plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()
